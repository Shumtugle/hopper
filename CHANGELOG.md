# Changes

## 1.7

- Settings, three chalk buttons along the bottom of the hopscotch: sound or no
  sound; the watcher up to the twentieth level or up to the hundredth; hard or
  easy. Easy helps the finger: a hop ordered into harm is turned aside with a
  warning tap, and a spring that would throw the drop into the liquid only
  bounces it.

## 1.6.4

- The watcher no longer gives up the fight with the black drop at its first
  strike: it plays on over the cut world to the end.

## 1.6.3

- The black drop comes from a tower: a tall dark shaft far beyond the arena,
  ribbed, spiked and crowned with red fire, straight ahead at the start. It
  leaps from the top in one long arc, and after a cut it comes again the same way.
- The watcher plays the black drop too: up onto a slab, wait, come down on it as
  its rim flares, and get down and away when it climbs up.

## 1.6.2

- A strike can no longer be missed: a bright slash runs right across the screen
  along the line the world is cut by, with a flash, and the drop itself is cut,
  sawn through like the islands when the eye looks down on it. A thin seam stays
  on the view for the rest of the try: one strike left. The second strike slashes
  across the first.

## 1.6.1

- The black drop's level is drawn by hand: open ground and two slabs above it in
  the corners, each reached by a spring, their edges open over the ground.
- It stops to look up only where the drop could really come down on it, and its
  rim flares while it does: the moment to jump. It plays a little slower, and
  coming down on it counts a little more generously.

## 1.6

- The black drop: the hundredth level is a fight. It comes for you by your own
  rules; landing on your island it strikes and cuts the world in two, the half
  without you tipping into the black and the cut islands showing their steps in
  section. Two strikes and you are lost. Come down on it from a floor above to
  beat it; right under you it stops and looks up.
- The end: beaten, it leaves the world you grew in gold under a rose dawn, the
  game won and the levels going on. A hopscotch mark then stands between the map
  and the eye: the open mode, every level and every world.
- The open mode shows the world of each number behind the hopscotch.

## 1.5.1

- Rivals are clumsier: slower, more often in doubt, more often in the liquid,
  and they drop in a little later.

## 1.5

- Rivals: from the thirty-sixth level on, about one level in four brings a
  lilac drop racing you to the gate, two from the sixtieth. It plays the same
  task about as well as a person: it hesitates, steps aside, slips into the
  liquid and drops in again from above at the start. Heard landing near you,
  shown on the map, brighter once it has all its shards. First through the gate
  wins; if it is the rival, the level starts again.
- The developer's mode: a long press on the hopscotch opens every level, makes
  the strip scroll fast, writes nothing down, and grows the world in the dark
  to the size of the level's number.

## 1.4

- The world: every level won for the first time grows a piece of it out of the
  black, in the dark before the next level, with a bell and a ring on the
  liquid. Crystals first, growing out of each other; then glades, branches and
  trees, cubes, houses with lit windows, streets, towers. Now and then a
  precious thing: a burning gem, a funnel of light, a prism, with bells of its
  own. A tap sends the world under, and the level wakes.

## 1.3.1

- Mirages read as mirrors. Over each stands an upright sheet of quicksilver,
  always turned to the drop, and in it the drop itself bounces, wavering as if
  seen through hot air. The ball that used to bounce on a mirage is gone.
- Near a mirage, the island ahead answers every landing with a soft note. A
  mirage keeps silent, as the liquid does.

## 1.3

- Order levels: rare, from the twenty-eighth on. Their shards must be taken in
  a hidden order. Each one hums when the drop is near, the first lowest and
  slowest, each next one higher and quicker.
- A shard taken out of turn opens its island into a funnel. The drop goes down
  into one of the recent levels already won; win it, and the funnel pours the
  drop back to the level it fell from.

## 1.2.1

- A shift you cannot miss: the ground trembles harder and the eye with it,
  three diamonds count the last landings down, and as the old act goes under
  the eye rises over the board to watch the new one come up, then returns.
- Every act wears a colour of its own, and its number is shown when it stands.

## 1.2

- Shifting levels: from the sixteenth on, about one in four comes in two or
  three acts. Each act lasts a number of landings; three before its end the
  ground trembles, then everything but the island under the drop goes down into
  the liquid and the next act rises, grown from that island. Shards left behind
  move on to the next act; only the last act has the gate.
- The watcher plays shifting levels too, act by act.

## 1.1.1

- The watcher plays at a finger's pace, and its eye sits in the top right corner.

## 1.1

- The watcher: an eye beside the map. Tapped, it solves the level and plays it
  as a finger would; a swipe takes the drop back.
- The hopscotch: every level as a chalked square, without end. Any level played
  can be played again; the next one waits, lit. The watcher's levels carry its
  mark. Opened after the watcher's game and by the back button.
- Progress keeps the highest level reached and who won each one.

## 1.0

The first release.

- A drop of quicksilver seen from inside, bouncing on its own; swipes order the
  next bounce: a step, a leap, a corner, a turn.
- Islands of bismuth over a black liquid: shards, gate, glass, springs with an
  arrow, hot plates and beads, mirages that show the drop its reflection.
- Floors one over another: hop under them, be thrown onto them, fall from them.
- Ten levels drawn by hand, then levels grown from their number without end,
  each checked by a solver; a climb of difficulty with breathers.
- Each level rises out of total black at a tap; a win goes up into white and
  slowly out to black.
- A map in the corner that turns with the drop and shows every floor.
- All sound synthesised: bells under water, dry knocks, a low string.
